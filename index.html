About

I'm a Master's student in Computational Material Science at TU Bergakademie Freiberg, currently writing my thesis at Carl ZEISS AG.

My work sits at the intersection of optics and machine learning: I benchmark physics-informed and operator-based deep learning surrogates (CNNs, Fourier Neural Operators, Maxwell-residual networks) for full-wave electromagnetic simulation of large-scale optical metasurfaces. Before that I built metalens design pipelines and ray tracers at ZEISS, trained surrogate models for RCWA/FDTD at PlanOpSim, and classified semiconductor defects from acoustic microscopy signals at TU Freiberg.

Earlier, I spent a year as a software engineer at LTIMindtree — so I care about clean, traceable code as much as I care about the physics.

Metalens Lab

A tiny, live version of what I do at work. A plane wave hits a metasurface whose nano-pillars imprint a hyperbolic phase profile φ(x) = −k(√(x² + f²) − f). Every pillar re-radiates as a Huygens source and the field below is computed in your browser by summing those wavelets. Change the parameters and watch the focus move.

Tip: detune the illumination from the design wavelength to see chromatic aberration — the focus shifts, just like in real metalenses.
